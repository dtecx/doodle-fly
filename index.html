How it works

Scientists sliced a fruit fly’s brain into 7,000 layers, imaged each one with an electron microscope and traced every neuron and synapse. That wiring diagram, the FlyWire connectome, is public. Doodle Fly loads all of it into your browser and simulates every neuron spike by spike, with the leaky integrate-and-fire model of Shiu et al. (2024).

The game finds the platform the fly can still reach; its direction becomes input to the LC10a neurons on that side, small-object detectors a fly uses to chase a target. The signal travels through the brain on its own. Six types of descending neurons, the brain’s cables to the body, decide the move: more spikes on the right than on the left means right. Nobody taught it: the left/right decision comes out of the wiring.
